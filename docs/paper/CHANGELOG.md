# Changelog: first version (Aug 2023) -> revision (Nov 2026)

Everything not listed here (Introduction, Literature Review, references 1-26, the biological rationale, ΔWnt = 0.41859,
ΔCCL5 = 0.40467, the four conditions, 900 inputs / 4 hidden layers / 100 sessions) is unchanged. The first version was a summer
project and, apart from this repository, was not published elsewhere.

## Claims that no longer hold (old claim -> new claim)

| # | Where | Old | New | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Abstract, Discussion p.19, Conclusion p.21 | "the perturbed networks failed to classify images as accurately as the control"; "a defined statistical difference in how well this neural network will classify images"; "a statistically lower loss of image accuracy for the control" | Perturbed networks **learn more slowly** (higher area under the training-loss curve, more sessions to reach a fixed loss). Held-out classification accuracy is **near chance (25%) in every condition** and does not differ between conditions. | The old testing code took the argmax of the *input* layer and compared `.all()` values, so "accuracy" (a constant 75% with 4 classes) did not measure the network; the first paper's own Limitations section said accuracy was "similar in all four networks". With the corrected test, accuracy is near chance because there are only 3 training images per class. |
| 2 | Data Analysis, Results (Figs 2-5), Discussion p.18-19 | Rate of loss decrease judged from the coefficient b of y = a - b ln(x): "With a smaller number, the loss decreases faster"; "both networks with the perturbed Wnt ... have a smaller ln(x) coefficient" | Learning speed is measured directly: area under the training-loss curve and sessions to reach a fixed loss, per seed, with ANOVA. Curve-fit analysis removed. | For y = a - b ln(x), a *larger* b is a steeper fall, and the reported Wnt coefficients (1.11, 1.15) were larger, not smaller, than the others (0.71, 0.57). The interpretation was backwards. |
| 3 | Results p.15 | "All population sizes are n = 36, fulfilling the central limit theorem"; one-tailed t-tests on end-of-run mean loss | n = 20 independently seeded networks per condition and image set; two-way ANOVA (Wnt, CCL5, Wnt x CCL5), Welch one-tailed contrasts with Holm correction, effect sizes with CIs | The unit of the 36 samples was not defined and they were not independent networks. |
| 4 | Discussion p.19 | Control "reach[es] a low level of loss and had the greatest relative decrease" (control final loss 0.31) | Same direction, new numbers (Table 1); at the original learning rate of 0.1 the corrected network does not learn stably in any condition (Supplementary S1-S2). | Training was un-shuffled (class-blocked), so the old loss mostly reflected tracking the most recent class. |
| 5 | Conclusion p.21 | "By empirically showing that the model can accurately reflect the signaling in ASD, Wnt and CCL5 are shown to be major players in the system." | Removed. | Not supported: the model shows a computational effect of the modeled perturbations, not that the molecules are major players in ASD. |
| 6 | Discussion / Future Research / Conclusion | "emergent properties that were not seen with either on their own" (no test) | Wnt x CCL5 interaction tested directly (2 x 2 ANOVA): significant for the AUC, final loss and sessions-to-chance-loss; size depends on the scale (permutation test and log-AUC are less decisive) | The claim is now tested; no biological crosstalk is modeled. |
